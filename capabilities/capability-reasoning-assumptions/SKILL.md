# Capability Reasoning Assumptions

Assess a real subject by separating necessary constraints from inherited convention, then rebuilding the answer from what remains. This is analysis only. Read actual evidence, verify unfamiliar facts, and label unknowns; laws, logic, measured behavior, and binding contracts may constrain the result, while convention remains a hypothesis. Stop when further reduction cannot change the decision.

## Procedure

1. Frame the information need, job to be done, or claim under test. Name the candidate and material constraints.
2. List assumptions and classify each as real, inherited, or unknown, with evidence and the cost of being wrong.
3. Reduce real constraints into independent, decision-relevant primitives. Preserve a convention only when its purpose remains justified.
4. Rebuild the explanation, design, or decision from those primitives. Compare viable alternatives where they affect the result, then recommend the smallest reversible choice when evidence is sufficient.
5. Give each decision-relevant primitive an observable check: metric or acceptance condition, evidence source, and goal-relative threshold when known.
6. Return the frame, ledger, primitives, rebuilt result, discarded conventions, and changing evidence. For a consequential unresolved choice, state options, recommendation, impact, and the exact decision needed. Compare it with the DoD; repair authorized analysis errors and repeat affected checks. Use independent review and critique perspectives for material contested plans or critiques when available, otherwise sequentially and report the limit. Missing evidence or authority is incomplete with its next check.

## Definition of Done

- The subject, decision, and material constraints are explicit.
- Each material assumption is classified with evidence, uncertainty, and consequence.
- The rebuilt result follows from decision-relevant primitives and names observable checks for material claims.
- The output is an evidence-based assessment, not implementation authority.
